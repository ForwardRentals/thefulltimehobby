Property photography for Squamish

Squamish listings sell on setting. The Stawamus Chief, the estuary, the river and the mountains around the valley are part of what a buyer is paying for, so a good listing shoot shows the house and where it sits. I plan each shoot around the light on that particular property, which is easier when you know how the sun moves around this valley.

The town is also growing fast, with new townhome, duplex and condo projects across Garibaldi Highlands and downtown. Builders and agents selling new product need photos that make a fresh build look finished and inviting, and aerials that show the neighbourhood around it.

Drone photography in Squamish

Squamish gives drone photography plenty to work with: granite walls, the Howe Sound estuary, forest and river. It also comes with real rules. Much of the best terrain is inside provincial parks such as the Chief and Shannon Falls, where drone launches need permission, and wind funnels up Howe Sound on many afternoons. I check airspace, land access and the weather window before every flight.

For development and construction clients I can document a site from first foundation to finished building, and shoot view studies from the height of each floor. See the full drone photography page.

Squamish real estate & drone photography FAQ

Do you live in Squamish?

Yes. I’m based here, which means local knowledge of the light and the neighbourhoods, and no travel in your quote for Squamish listings.

Can you photograph a new-build show home or presale unit?

Yes. It’s a big part of my Squamish work. I can also add exteriors, aerials and view studies for the whole project.

Can you shoot acreage or properties outside town?

Yes. Squamish Valley and Paradise Valley properties are a short drive, and aerials are especially useful on large lots.

Are drone flights allowed everywhere in Squamish?

No. Provincial parks, airspace and private property all matter. I confirm what’s possible at your location before booking.
