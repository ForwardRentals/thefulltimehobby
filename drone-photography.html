Airspace, parks and permission: the rules I plan around

Flying a drone commercially in Canada isn’t just pointing and shooting. Before I book a flight I check:

  • Transport Canada rules for the type of flight, including distance from people and whether it’s a basic or advanced operation
  • Airspace, since controlled airspace needs authorization through NAV CANADA
  • Parks and public land, as many provincial parks restrict drone launches without a permit
  • Landowner permission and privacy for the property and neighbours

If a location isn’t workable, I’ll tell you up front and suggest another way to get the shot. Details in my guide to flying a drone in the Sea to Sky.

Drone photography FAQ

Where do you offer drone photography?

I’m based in Squamish and fly across the Sea to Sky corridor: Squamish, Whistler, Pemberton, Lions Bay, Britannia Beach, Furry Creek and Mount Currie, plus Vancouver and the North Shore by arrangement. Every location is checked for airspace and access first.

How much does drone photography cost?

It depends on the location, the number of shots or the length of video you need, and whether it’s a single flight or a series like construction progress. Send me the address or site and what you’re trying to capture and I’ll send a quote.

Do I need permission to fly a drone in the Sea to Sky?

You need to follow Transport Canada’s drone rules, get permission from the landowner, and respect airspace and park restrictions. Many provincial parks restrict drone launches without a permit, and flying in controlled airspace or close to people needs additional authorization. I check all of this before booking a flight, and I’ll tell you honestly if a location isn’t feasible. See my guide to flying a drone in the Sea to Sky.

Can you fly in Whistler Village or near the ski hill?

Often it’s possible, but it depends on airspace, bystanders and the specific location, and sometimes it needs extra authorization. Tell me where and when, and I’ll confirm what’s feasible before you commit.

What camera do you use?

A DJI Mavic 3 with a four-thirds Hasselblad camera, shooting 20 megapixel RAW stills and high-resolution video. That means clean files in low light, strong dynamic range for bright skies and dark forest, and detail that holds up in print.

What is a view study?

A view study is a set of photos taken from the exact height of each floor of a planned or under-construction building, facing the same direction. It lets buyers see the view from the 1st, 3rd or 5th floor before the building is finished. It’s a popular tool for presale marketing.

Can you document construction progress?

Yes. I fly the same vantage points on a schedule, weekly, monthly or at key milestones, so you get a consistent visual record for investors, lenders, marketing and your own archive. The crane and framing images on this page show construction stages at a Squamish development.

Can you combine drone work with real estate photos or video?

Yes, and it’s the best way to book. Interior and exterior stills, drone aerials and video can all happen on the same day. See my real estate photography page.
