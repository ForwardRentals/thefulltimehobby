How to Prepare Your Home for Real Estate Photos (Squamish & Whistler Checklist)

By Jeremy Allen · November 18, 2026

A room-by-room checklist for getting your home ready before the photographer arrives, plus the Sea to Sky details that are easy to miss: hot tubs, mudrooms, bins and the view out the window.

Why prep matters more than the camera

The most expensive gear in the world can’t photograph clutter away. A home that’s ready to shoot looks bigger, brighter and more valuable in photos, and in the Sea to Sky, where buyers are often scrolling listings from another city, those photos are the first showing. Here’s the checklist I give agents and owners before I arrive for real estate photography in Squamish, Whistler and Pemberton.

The day before

  • Clean the windows. In a mountain town the windows are the view. Smudges and dust show up in every wide-angle shot.
  • Check every light bulb. Replace burnt-out bulbs and use bulbs of the same colour temperature throughout, so the home doesn’t look patchy.
  • Do the laundry and dishes. Nothing in the sink, nothing drying on racks, no laundry piles.
  • Plan for pets. Pet beds, bowls and litter boxes should be out of sight, and ideally the pets are out of the house during the shoot.

Room by room

Kitchen

Clear nearly everything off the counters. Leave one or two intentional items, like a bowl of fruit or a plant. Put away the dish soap, sponge, dish rack and small appliances, and tuck magnets and notes off the fridge.

Living room and bedrooms

Straighten cushions, fold throws, make beds with the tidiest linens you have, and clear surfaces. Take down very personal items like family photos so buyers can picture themselves living there. Remove cords and clutter from nightstands.

Bathrooms

Close toilet lids, hide toiletries and towels in use, and put out clean, matching towels. Clear the tub and shower of bottles.

Entries, closets and storage

Clear the mudroom. In the Sea to Sky, that means boots, skis, snowboards, bikes, helmets and packs stored away or neatly arranged. Keep closets tidy in case they’re photographed, because storage is a selling point.

Sea to Sky-specific things to check

  • Hot tub and deck. Clean and cover off, furniture arranged, snow cleared in winter.
  • Driveway and curb. Park cars on the street or in the garage, and clear snow, leaves or construction debris.
  • Garbage, recycling and compost bins. Put them out of sight, bear-safe and away from the front of the house.
  • Gear and clutter outside. Roof boxes, kayaks, bikes and the trailer all look better somewhere else.
  • The view. Trim anything blocking a mountain view and open every blind and curtain.

On the day

  • Turn on every light in the house, including lamps and under-cabinet lights.
  • Open all blinds and curtains.
  • Turn off ceiling fans and TVs, and put the fireplace in a neutral state.
  • Let me know about any problem areas or features you want highlighted.

If you’re selling a new build, a show home or a rental, I can also shoot drone aerials to show the lot, the neighbourhood and the mountain views from above. They work best when the exterior is as ready as the interior.

What about staging?

Professional staging helps, and the show home photos on my real estate photography page are a good example. But you don’t need a stager to get a big improvement. Decluttering, cleaning and good light do most of the work.
